# Positioning Framework — Question Bank

Build a 4-step positioning framework: Why Now → Why This → Why You → Convergence. Each step has opening, deepening, and pushing-past-surface questions. Ask one at a time. Wait for the full answer. Push when it's surface-level.

---

## Step 1: Why Now — The Forces Driving Change

These questions surface the market forces that make the client's work urgent and timely. The goal: identify 2-3 named forces or shifts that create urgency for the audience.

### Opening

- "What's happening right now — in your industry, in your clients' lives, in the broader culture — that makes what you do more necessary than it was five years ago?"
- "If you had to explain to a smart friend who knows nothing about your field why this moment matters, what would you say?"

### Deepening

- "You mentioned [force]. What's the second-order effect of that? What does it cause that most people in your space aren't talking about?"
- "Is there a collision happening — two or three trends slamming into each other that creates a new kind of problem? What are those trends?"
- "What are your clients dealing with right now that they couldn't even name two years ago?"
- "Who's getting hurt by this shift? And who doesn't realize they're about to get hurt?"

### Pushing Past Surface

- "I hear you, but honestly, a lot of people in your space could say that too. What's the piece that only YOU see? The angle most of your peers are missing?"
- "If you had to name this moment — like 'The Great Resignation' or 'The Burnout Epidemic' — what would you call it? What's the phenomenon?"
- "What's the thing your audience is doing right now that feels like the right move but is actually making things worse?"

### What You're Building

By the end of Step 1, you should have:
- 2-3 named forces or shifts (e.g., "The Knowledge Economy Collapse", "The AI Saviour Trap")
- A sense of urgency — why NOW, not later
- Optionally, a named phenomenon that ties the forces together
- Specifics, not abstractions — particular industries, behaviors, or misconceptions affected

---

## Step 2: Why This — The Contrarian Position

These questions surface the beliefs that set the client apart from their peers. The goal: articulate what everyone else gets wrong and what the client's approach does differently.

### Opening

- "What does everyone in your industry believe that you think is wrong — or at least dangerously incomplete?"
- "If you were at a dinner party with 10 other experts in your field, what's the thing you'd say that would make half the table uncomfortable?"

### Deepening

- "You said [contrarian view]. Why do most people get this wrong? What's the root of the misconception?"
- "What's the approach that sounds good in theory — that your peers teach — but that you've seen fail over and over in practice?"
- "If a prospective client came to you after working with someone else in your space, what damage would you expect to find? What does the conventional approach break?"
- "What's the thing you wish you could put on a billboard? The one-sentence truth that would save your audience years of wasted effort?"

### Pushing Past Surface

- "That's a strong position. But can you ground it? Tell me about a specific client where the conventional approach failed and your approach worked. What happened?"
- "If someone pushed back on that position — said you were wrong — what's your evidence? What have you seen that makes you so sure?"
- "Is there a name for what you do differently? Not your offer name — the methodology, the approach, the thing itself. What would you call it?"

### What You're Building

By the end of Step 2, you should have:
- 1-3 contrarian positions clearly articulated (what's wrong, why it's wrong, what's right instead)
- The conventional approach named and its failure mode described
- Evidence grounding each position (specific client stories, market data, observed patterns)
- Optionally, a named methodology or approach (a "mindshare word")

---

## Step 3: Why You — The Proof

These questions surface the evidence that the client is the right person to deliver on their contrarian position. The goal: build a proof case that goes beyond credentials.

### Opening

- "Forget your bio for a second. What have you actually done, built, or survived that makes you the person to solve this problem?"
- "If someone asked your best client why they chose you over everyone else, what would they say?"

### Deepening

- "What's a result you've gotten for a client that even surprised you? Something that exceeded what you thought was possible?"
- "Is there something in your background — maybe something that seems unrelated — that actually gives you an edge no one else in your space has?"
- "What's the longest you've worked with a single client? What kept them coming back?"
- "What's the proof that's hard to argue with? Not a testimonial — a number, a transformation, a before-and-after that speaks for itself."
- "Do you have a personal story — an origin moment — where you realized this was your work? What happened?"

### Pushing Past Surface

- "You mentioned [credential/result]. I believe it, but I want to make it land harder. Can you zoom into the specific moment — the before state, what happened, the after state? Make me see the transformation."
- "What would a skeptic need to hear to believe you? What's the evidence for the person who doesn't already trust you?"
- "What's the unique combination you have that no one else can assemble? Not one skill — the specific stack of experiences that intersects in you?"

### What You're Building

By the end of Step 3, you should have:
- 2-4 proof categories (transformation stories, unique background, specific results, the origin story)
- Each proof point grounded in specifics (numbers, moments, before-and-after)
- The "unique stack" — the combination of experiences that only this person has
- An origin story that earns the right to make the contrarian claims from Step 2

---

## Step 4: Convergence — The Vision

These questions surface where all the positioning comes together into a single conviction. The goal: one sentence that ties Why Now + Why This + Why You into a vision.

### Opening

- "When everything you've told me comes together — the urgency, your contrarian view, your proof — what does it point to? What's the world you're trying to create for your clients?"
- "If every client you worked with in the next five years got exactly the outcome you're promising, what would be true about their lives that isn't true now?"

### Deepening

- "Is there a single sentence — a conviction — that ties all of this together? Something like 'I believe that...' What's the statement?"
- "What's the name of what you do? Not your job title — the thing itself. If your methodology or approach had a name, what would it be?"
- "When you look at Why Now + Why This + Why You, what's the ONE thing that only you can say? The intersection that no one else occupies?"

### What You're Building

By the end of Step 4, you should have:
- A convergence statement — one sentence that captures the full positioning
- A vision — what becomes true for clients when the positioning works
- Optionally, a name for the methodology or approach
- The sense that all three steps resolve into this single point
