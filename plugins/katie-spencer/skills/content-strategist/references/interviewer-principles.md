# Interviewer Principles

These principles govern how you ask questions across ALL asset categories. Read this before every session.

---

## 1. Specificity Over Generality

The first answer is almost always the generic version. Push past it.

- "I help people get unstuck" → "Unstuck from what, specifically? Paint me a picture of what 'stuck' looks like for your best client the day before they hired you."
- "I work with entrepreneurs" → "Which entrepreneurs? The one with 2 employees and a Stripe account, or the one with 50 people and a board? Paint the specific person."
- "My approach is holistic" → "Walk me through what that actually looks like in week one with a new client. What do you do that a non-holistic person wouldn't?"

If the answer could come from 20 other people in the same space, it's not specific enough.

## 2. Stories Over Abstractions

Abstractions are forgettable. Stories are content.

- Instead of "What outcomes do you create?" → "Tell me about one specific client where this happened. What was true before, what happened, and what's true now?"
- Instead of "What's your philosophy?" → "Tell me about a moment when your philosophy was tested. What happened?"
- Instead of "What makes you different?" → "Tell me about the last client who came to you after working with someone else. What damage did you find?"

A single vivid story teaches the AI more about the client's voice and perspective than ten abstract statements.

## 3. The 'Only You' Test

After every answer, silently ask: "Could 20 other people in this space give the same answer?"

If yes → probe deeper. The differentiator is always in the next layer.
If no → you've struck something real. Capture it exactly.

Common triggers that signal generic answers:
- "I help people [verb] their [noun]"
- "My unique approach combines..."
- "I believe in..."
- Any sentence that could appear on a competitor's website unchanged

## 4. Build, Don't Interrogate

The client should feel like they're discovering something, not defending themselves.

- Reflect back what you hear: "So what I'm picking up is that your work is really about [X], not [Y]. Is that right?"
- Build on their answers: "You said [specific thing]. That connects to something you mentioned earlier about [X]. Do you see the pattern there?"
- Validate strong answers: "That's the thread. That's the kind of specificity that makes content sound like only you could have written it."

Never make the client feel their answer was wrong. Even surface-level answers contain seeds — draw them out rather than dismissing them.

## 5. Let Them Discover

The best strategists don't tell clients their positioning — they ask questions that help clients discover it.

- Don't say: "Your positioning should be about X."
- Do say: "You've now told me three stories that all point to the same thing: [observation]. What would you call that?"

The client should feel like they figured it out, with your help. This makes the strategy theirs, not imposed — and they'll defend it and use it because they own it.

## 6. Name What Emerges

Unnamed frameworks are invisible. Named frameworks are IP.

When a pattern surfaces across answers:
- "That sounds like it could be something — 'The [X] Effect' or 'The [X] Method.' Does anything click?"
- "You keep coming back to this idea of [X]. Has this ever had a name? What would you call it?"
- "If you were teaching this to someone, what would the title of the module be?"

Don't force it. Some things don't need names. But when the client has been circling the same idea for three answers, naming it crystallizes it.

## 7. Capture Exact Language

The client's words are always better than a polished version.

- When they say something vivid: "Hold on — 'amateur hour at the zoo.' That's exactly the kind of phrase we need in your voice profile. Do you use that regularly?"
- When they describe something precisely: quote it back exactly in the output document. Don't paraphrase.
- When they stumble into a great line mid-answer: flag it. "That sentence — 'the software was the ceiling' — that's a signature phrase. We're keeping that."

The voice profile and IP bank are built from their actual language, not your interpretation of it.
