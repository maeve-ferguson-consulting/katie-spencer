# Voice Profile — Question Bank

Build a voice profile deep enough that AI can write in the client's voice and the client barely needs to edit. Most voice profiles stop at "warm, professional, authoritative." That's useless. We need the specific patterns, anti-patterns, and contextual rules that make the voice unmistakably theirs.

Work through 3 phases: The Sound → Anti-Patterns → Contextual Rules.

**If existing content is available:** Ask the client to share 2-3 pieces they're proud of (newsletters, social posts, talks). Analyze before asking questions — it's easier to confirm patterns than to describe them from scratch.

---

## Phase A: The Sound

### Opening

- "Read me one of your favorite things you've ever written — or tell me about a piece where you thought 'that sounds exactly like me.' What was it?"
- "If your writing were a person at a party, how would they talk? Are they the one telling stories in the corner? The one who says the uncomfortable truth? The one who makes everyone laugh and then drops something serious?"

### Deepening

- "Do you have signature phrases — things you say so often your clients could finish the sentence? What are they?"
- "What's the energy of your best writing? Is it fast and punchy, or slow and deliberate? Warm or sharp? Casual or precise? Pick the words that feel right."
- "How do you open a piece? Do you start with a story, a question, a bold statement, an observation? What's your instinct?"
- "How do you close? Soft invitation? Direct ask? Provocative question that lingers? A statement that lands like a full stop?"
- "What about profanity, humor, vulnerability? How far do you go? What's in bounds and what's not?"
- "Do you use emojis? Exclamation points? Ellipses? ALL CAPS for emphasis? What are your formatting habits?"

### Pushing Past Surface

- "You said 'warm and direct.' Can you give me a sentence that sounds like you at your most warm? And one that sounds like you at your most direct?"
- "If I read 10 random sentences from your writing, what would make me recognize them as yours? What's the fingerprint?"
- "What three words would your audience use to describe your voice? And what three words would they NEVER use?"

## Phase B: Anti-Patterns (What Your Voice Is NOT)

This is the section most voice profiles miss entirely. It's the difference between "sounds vaguely like them" and "sounds exactly like them."

### The Six Ghostwriting Traps

Present these one at a time. For each, ask whether it applies to the client:

**1. The Compression Trap**
"Sometimes a writer takes a long, textured personal story and crushes it into one polished paragraph. The mess disappears — but the mess is where personality lives. When you tell a personal story in your content, do you need the mess? The detours, the weird details, the things that didn't go to plan?"

**2. The Front-Load Trap**
"Some writers put all the personal stuff at the top and then disappear into pure strategy for the rest. Do you weave personal throughout, or do you compartmentalize — personal here, strategic there?"

**3. The Clean-Up Trap**
"Writers instinctively clean up the weird specific details — the exact brand of coffee, the phrase your kid used, the temperature of the room. But those odd details are your fingerprints. Are you a details person? Do the specific odd details matter in your voice?"

**4. The Signposting Trap**
"Some writing has structural scaffolding: 'Let me say that again,' 'Here's the thing,' 'This is important.' Does that sound like you? Or do you just say the thing without narrating your own structure?"

**5. The Parenthetical Trap**
"Do you think in parentheses, asides, mid-sentence qualifications? Things like '(honestly, it drove me a little crazy)' or '(not to mention the timing was absurd).' If yes, those are part of your voice. A writer who cleans them out is removing your thought patterns."

**6. The Soft-Close Trap**
"When it's time to mention your offer or invite people to work with you, are you direct about it? Or do you soften and hint? Some writers instinctively soften the commercial ask because selling feels 'off-brand.' But some people are actually quite direct about their offers. Which are you?"

### Broader Anti-Patterns

- "Think about content you've read that made you cringe — either your own or someone else's. What specifically felt wrong? What made it feel inauthentic?"
- "If someone were ghostwriting for you and they nailed the words but missed the feel, what would be off? What would tip you off that it's not really you?"
- "Can you give me a before-and-after? Something where someone (or AI) rewrote your work and you could feel the difference. What did they change that they shouldn't have?"

## Phase C: Contextual Rules

### Format-Specific Voice

- "Does your voice change depending on the format? Are you different in newsletters vs. social posts vs. long-form articles? How?"
- "Is there a difference between your 'teaching' voice and your 'selling' voice? How would I know which mode you're in?"
- "What about when the topic is heavy — grief, failure, vulnerability? Does your voice get quieter, more direct, more raw, something else?"
- "How do you handle humor? Is it part of your regular voice, or does it come out only in certain contexts?"

### Spelling and Formatting

- "American or British English spelling? Most clients are US-based, so we'll default to American unless you prefer otherwise."
- "Do you use em-dashes, or do you prefer commas and semicolons?"
- "Headers and bullet points in newsletters, or continuous prose?"
- "How long are your typical pieces? 500 words? 1,500? 3,000?"

---

## What You're Building

By the end, you should have:
- Specific energy and tone descriptors with examples
- Signature phrases the client actually uses (quoted exactly)
- Anti-patterns — at least 3-6 things the voice is NOT, with explanations
- Contextual rules for different formats and topics
- Formatting preferences (spelling, punctuation, structure)
- Ideally, before/after examples showing "this sounds like me" vs "this doesn't"
