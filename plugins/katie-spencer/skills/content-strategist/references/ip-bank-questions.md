# IP Bank — Question Bank

Build a catalog of the client's intellectual property: the named frameworks, signature stories, metaphors, proof points, and proprietary language that make their content theirs. Without an IP Bank, every piece of content starts from scratch. With one, the writer has a library to draw from.

Work through 4 phases: Frameworks → Stories → Metaphors & Language → Proof Points.

---

## Phase A: Frameworks and Models

### Opening

- "Do you have a process, model, or framework you walk clients through? Something with steps, pillars, or phases? Even if it's not named yet?"
- "When you explain your work to someone, do you ever draw something? A diagram, a sequence, a before-and-after? What does it look like?"

### Deepening

- "Walk me through the steps of how you work with a client from start to finish. Not the logistics — the transformation. What are the stages they go through?"
- "Do you have names for any of these stages, concepts, or methods? Things like 'The Discovery Phase' or 'The Alignment Check' — anything you've named, even informally?"
- "What are the 3-5 concepts you teach most often? The ideas you find yourself repeating because they're foundational?"
- "Do you have any diagnostic tools, assessments, or exercises you use with clients? Things that have a structure someone could follow?"

### Pushing Past Surface

- "You described [concept]. Could anyone in your space teach that, or is there something about YOUR version that's different? What makes your version yours?"
- "Is there something you teach that you've never seen anyone else teach? The thing where you think 'I can't believe no one else talks about this'?"
- "If a concept doesn't have a name yet, let's name it now. What would you call [the thing they just described]?"

### What to Capture for Each Framework

- **Name** (existing or newly created)
- **What it is** — 1-2 sentence description
- **Steps or elements** — the structure
- **When to reference in content** — what topics or situations naturally invoke this framework

---

## Phase B: Stories

### Opening

- "What are the stories you tell over and over? The ones that always land — in client calls, on stage, in your content?"
- "Do you have an origin story — the moment you realized this was your work? What happened?"

### Deepening

- "What about client transformation stories? Give me your top three — the ones that prove your method works."
- "For each story: what was true before, what happened, and what's true now? Paint the before-and-after."
- "Do you have any cautionary tales — stories about what happens when people do it the conventional way?"
- "Any stories from your life outside work that you use as metaphors? The climbing trip, the kitchen disaster, the thing your kid said?"
- "Do you have a 'worst moment that turned into the best thing' story? Something that gives you credibility because you've been through it?"

### Pushing Past Surface

- "That's a good story. Now make it land harder — give me the specific moment. What were you looking at? What did they say? What did it feel like?"
- "Can this client be named, or do they need to be anonymized? What level of detail can we use?"
- "Is there a story you've been wanting to tell but haven't found the right moment for? What's been sitting in your notes?"

### What to Capture for Each Story

- **Name/shorthand** — a reference label (e.g., "The Sarah turnaround", "The conference elevator moment")
- **The story** — brief version with key details
- **The lesson** — what it teaches or proves
- **Best used when** — what content context it fits

---

## Phase C: Metaphors and Language

### Metaphors

- "Do you have metaphors you lean on? Things like 'building the plane while flying it' or 'your business is a garden, not a machine'?"
- "When you explain a complex concept, what comparison do you reach for?"
- "Are there metaphors from outside your field that you bring in? Sports, cooking, architecture, nature?"

### Proprietary Language

- "Are there words or phrases you've coined or repurposed? Things your audience would recognize as uniquely yours?"
- "Do you have insider terms — language that people inside your world use that outsiders don't?"
- "What about language you've reclaimed or redefined? Words that mean something different in your context?"

### Language to Avoid

- "Are there phrases you would NEVER use? Industry jargon that makes you roll your eyes?"
- "Any words that feel overused in your space? Things everyone says that you consciously avoid?"
- "What about AI-typical language? 'In today's fast-paced world,' 'Let's dive in,' 'unlock your potential' — anything like that you'd cringe at?"

---

## Phase D: Proof Points

### Hard Evidence

- "What's your strongest piece of evidence? The number, the result, the transformation that's hardest to argue with?"
- "Do you have data — survey results, client outcomes, industry stats you cite regularly?"
- "What's the most impressive result you've helped a client achieve? Can we use the specifics?"

### Social Proof

- "What about testimonials, press mentions, case studies, speaking gigs, podcast appearances?"
- "Have you been quoted or featured anywhere that lends credibility?"
- "Do you have any third-party validation — certifications, awards, partnerships — that matters to your audience?"

### Proof Prioritization

- "If you had to prove your approach works to a skeptic in 60 seconds, which piece of evidence would you lead with?"
- "Which proof points do you use most often in sales conversations? Those are your go-to proof — they should show up in content."

### What to Capture for Each Proof Point

- **The proof** — specific data, result, or source
- **Context** — where it came from, how it can be used
- **Permission level** — can it be named? Needs anonymizing? Public?

---

## What You're Building

By the end, you should have:
- 2-5 named frameworks or models with structure described
- 5-10 signature stories tagged by use case
- A set of metaphors and proprietary language with usage context
- Language-to-avoid list
- 5+ proof points with specifics and permission levels
- Everything organized so the content writer can browse and draw from it
