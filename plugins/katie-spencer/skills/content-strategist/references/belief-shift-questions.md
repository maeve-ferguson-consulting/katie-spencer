# Belief Shift Bank — Question Bank

Build a catalog of wrong belief → right belief pairs that drive content angles. Every piece of content exists to move the audience from a wrong belief to a right belief. The belief shift is the invisible architecture underneath the story.

Work through 4 phases: Surface → Articulate → Map → Prioritize.

**Prerequisite:** The Positioning Framework should exist (even skeletal) before building Belief Shifts. The framework elements give you categories to organize shifts into. If no framework exists, build shifts generically and map them later.

---

## Phase A: Surface the Wrong Beliefs

### Opening

- "When someone comes to you for the first time, what do they believe that you know isn't true? What's the #1 misconception you have to undo before any real work can happen?"
- "Think about the last 5 clients who came to you. What were they trying to do that was actually making their problem worse?"

### Deepening

- "What's the wrong belief that's hardest to dislodge — the one that's so deeply held that people get defensive when you challenge it?"
- "Is there a wrong belief that sounds like good advice? Something that a well-meaning mentor or book might have told them that's actually keeping them stuck?"
- "What do your clients believe about [each element of their positioning framework]? For each step you outlined, what's the misconception your audience holds?"
- "What's the 'stage one' wrong belief — the thing brand new people believe? And what's the 'stage three' wrong belief — the thing people who've been working on this for a while still believe?"
- "If you overheard your ideal client talking to a friend about [topic], what would they say that would make you want to jump in and correct them?"

### Pushing Past Surface

- "You said they believe [X]. Where did that belief come from? What experience or authority taught them that?"
- "Is there a wrong belief that's so common it's basically industry standard? Something your peers also believe?"
- "What's the wrong belief that costs the most money? The most time? The most emotional energy?"

---

## Phase B: Articulate the Right Beliefs

For EACH wrong belief surfaced:

- "So if [wrong belief] is what they believe now, what do they need to believe instead? What's the replacement belief?"
- "Can you say the right belief in one sentence — something you'd actually say to a client? Not a theory, but the way you'd actually put it in conversation?"
- "What's the moment when the shift happens? Is there a single conversation, exercise, or experience where clients go from the old belief to the new one?"
- "What evidence makes the right belief stick? What proof do they need to see?"

### Format Each Shift As

> **They believe:** [Wrong belief — the specific thing they think]
> **They need to believe:** [Right belief — the specific replacement]
> **Why the wrong belief persists:** [What keeps it in place — cultural norm, bad advice, surface logic]
> **What shifts it:** [The moment, evidence, or insight that creates the shift]

---

## Phase C: Map to Framework

If the Positioning Framework exists:

- "Let's organize these. Looking at your positioning framework, which belief shifts belong under each step?"
- "For [Step 1: Why Now] — which wrong beliefs are about timing, urgency, or the market shift?"
- "For [Step 2: Why This] — which wrong beliefs are about the conventional approach vs. yours?"
- "For [Step 3: Why You] — which wrong beliefs are about who can solve this or what proof looks like?"
- "For [Convergence] — which wrong beliefs are about the big picture or the destination?"
- "Are there any belief shifts that don't fit neatly? Those might point to a positioning element you haven't articulated yet."

If no framework exists:

- "Let's group these by theme. Which beliefs cluster together? What categories emerge?"

---

## Phase D: Prioritize

- "Which 3 belief shifts would move the needle most this quarter? The ones that, if your audience shifted on these, they'd be ready to work with you?"
- "Are there belief shifts your audience needs to make before they're even ready to consume your content? Those are your gateway shifts — the first content someone should encounter."
- "Which beliefs can you shift through free content, and which only shift through paid work? Content belief shifts vs. offer belief shifts."
- "Of the content belief shifts, which ones are the most urgent right now? Based on what you're seeing in your audience's behavior or the market?"

---

## What You're Building

By the end, you should have:
- 10-30 belief shift pairs (wrong → right), numbered for reference
- Each mapped to a framework element (if framework exists)
- Tagged as content shift (address in free content) or offer shift (happens in paid work)
- Top 3-5 priority shifts identified for this quarter
- Enough shifts per framework element that the content planner can assign one per week without repeating within a month
